# Trading-simulation scorecard and the always-up baseline

Hand-authored 2026-08-11 from a `/grilling` session. Not generated by a build
script — see `kb/evaluation_spec.json`'s `note`.
Resolves [#14](https://github.com/jenujari/prdict-pov-v1/issues/14).

## Summary

- **Position**: direction from `sign(Σ predicted 10-step returns)`, size from a
  vol-normalized, capped, magnitude-proportional formula. Long/short.
- **Management**: single-position-at-a-time, fixed size until exit, exits on a
  sign flip or a drop into a neutral zone, same-day reversal allowed.
- **Costs**: 20 bps round trip (10 bps entry / 10 bps exit), linear in size.
- **Baseline**: literal buy-and-hold — one continuous long per window, entered
  once, one-way cost only.
- **Metrics**: Sharpe (annualized, net of costs) primary; cumulative return and
  max drawdown secondary; directional hit-rate and per-horizon RMSE diagnostic.
- **Claim**: risk-adjusted outperformance vs. buy-and-hold, net of costs — not
  an absolute-return race.
- **Reporting**: per-fold plus aggregate, on `kb/fold_spec.md`'s existing five
  folds and holdout, with 2008/2020 called out wherever they land.

Implemented at `prdict/evaluation.py`, parameters at `kb/evaluation_spec.json`.
Self-checked against synthetic series, not real predictions — #38/#39 haven't
trained a model yet. The state machine below is the protocol those tickets
score against, locked now so it cannot be shaped by a result it later produces.

## 1. Position rule

The model emits a predicted 10-step log-return vector at every origin
(`kb/target_spec.md`'s `y_1..y_10`). Direction is the sign of the predicted
**cumulative** 10-day return, `Σ y_pred_1..y_pred_10` — not `sign(y_pred_1)`,
which would throw away 9 of the 10 steps the model was actually trained to
predict and doesn't match the 10-day-hold framing the ticket itself poses
(point 2).

Size is magnitude-proportional and capped, not fixed-unit, so the model can
express how confident a call is rather than only its direction:

```
raw_signal = cum_return_pred / realized_vol_estimate
size       = 0                          if |raw_signal| < 0.1   (neutral zone)
           = clip(raw_signal, -1, 1)    otherwise
```

`realized_vol_estimate = std(daily log returns, trailing 60 sessions) * sqrt(10)`
— the same past-60 window already in every model's input schema (#11), scaled
by `sqrt(10)` so it sits on the same cumulative-10-day scale as the numerator
and the ratio is dimensionless. No separate scale constant `k` is introduced:
the ratio is already self-normalizing, and adding one would be a free
parameter nothing has asked for.

**Why vol-normalized, not a fixed linear scale.** A fixed `size = clip(k *
cum_return_pred, -1, 1)` produces very different effective risk in 2008 versus
a calm year, contaminating exactly the per-fold/per-regime comparison §5
exists to make visible. Normalizing by trailing realized vol targets roughly
constant risk per trade regardless of the current volatility regime.

**Why long/short, not long-only.** The instrument is Nifty index futures — no
physical short-sale friction — and capping the model to long-only would
quietly hand the always-up baseline an advantage in any regime where a short
call would have paid off, which is the opposite of a fair test of the
hypothesis under test (Nifty-return signal in planetary configuration).

**Neutral zone (0.1).** A genuinely free parameter — nothing upstream ties a
number to it. Chosen small and round: big enough that near-zero-conviction
noise doesn't force a trade, small enough to rarely gate a real directional
call. `#12`'s weak-but-real MI finding (max ~0.098 vs ~0.026 null) means most
raw signals will likely be modest, so this threshold matters and is recorded
rather than left implicit.

## 2. Position management — single-position-at-a-time

Only one position is ever open. Enter when flat and the signal is non-neutral;
hold at the entry-day size (never resized mid-trade — resizing on every
magnitude wobble is a materially bigger design commitment than anything asked
for here); exit when the fresh daily signal flips sign or falls back into the
neutral zone.

**Same-day reversal.** If the exit condition fires and that same origin's
fresh signal is already non-neutral in the opposite direction, the position
reverses immediately — pays both the exit leg and the new entry leg's cost the
same day, no cooldown. A cooldown would be another free parameter suppressing
cost the model actually incurs; §3's linear-in-size cost model already prices
whipsaw fairly.

**Why single-position over the rolling overlapping book that decisions 1–2
initially implied.** The ticket's own framing ("10-day hold means 10
concurrent positions") described the overlapping book as the default reading,
but the resolved design instead evaluates one book at a time, entered only
when flat — the overlapping-book alternative was considered and explicitly
rejected in favor of this policy (`#14` round 1).

**Window boundaries.** Positions are flattened at every fold/holdout boundary
and reopened under the incoming model — `kb/fold_spec.md` §6 already settles
this ("Positions are flattened at each fold boundary... the transaction costs
of doing so are charged"); this ticket does not reopen it, only inherits it.

## 3. Costs

20 bps round trip, from `kb/research/trading-cost-assumptions.md`: ~13 bps
hard cost (brokerage, STT, exchange/SEBI/stamp, GST on NSE Nifty futures at
2026 rates) plus ~7 bps slippage buffer for a liquid, low-impact instrument.
Locked as **10 bps on entry, 10 bps on exit**, scaled linearly by the trade's
position size (a half-size position pays half cost on that leg). A same-day
reversal is one exit leg (10 bps × old size) plus one entry leg (10 bps × new
size), not a single discounted round trip — it is mechanically two trades.

## 4. Baseline — always-up as literal buy-and-hold

Buy-and-hold is a single continuous long position, full size (1.0), entered
once at the start of each evaluation window (a fold's validation block, or the
holdout) and held to the window's end. It is **not** re-entered on the model's
single-position cadence.

Consequence: it pays the one-way entry cost (10 bps, same rate as
`costs.entry_bps`) once per window, and no exit cost until the same
window-boundary flatten the models are also charged for (§2). Forcing it to
round-trip on the models' cadence — paying 20 bps dozens of times a fold —
would understate a real buy-and-hold investor's return and hand the models an
unearned advantage, which runs backwards given how weak the signal already
looks (`#12`: max MI ~0.098 vs ~0.026 null). This is deliberately the
"genuinely hard bar" the ticket names (point 5): buy-and-hold pays trading
costs almost never, by construction.

## 5. Metrics and claim

| Tier | Metric |
|---|---|
| Primary | Sharpe ratio, annualized (`* sqrt(247)`, `kb/fold_spec.md`'s sessions/year), net of costs |
| Secondary | Cumulative return; max drawdown |
| Diagnostic | Directional hit-rate; per-horizon RMSE |

**Claim licensed: risk-adjusted outperformance vs. buy-and-hold, net of
costs** — not an absolute-return race. Over 2000–2026 buy-and-hold returned
roughly 30x (issue #14 body, point 5); beating that in absolute terms is
arguably unwinnable and not actually informative about whether astro-features
carry directional signal, which is the thing the project's hypothesis is
about (map `#2`). Risk-adjusted comparison is the fairer test, so it is the
number the write-up leans on; cumulative return and max drawdown are still
reported in full as required secondary context — nothing is hidden, only
de-emphasized.

## 6. Reporting

Per-fold **and** aggregate, reusing `kb/fold_spec.md`'s five rolling folds and
final holdout exactly (`kb/fold_spec.json`) — no new fold geometry, no new
purge. 2008 and 2020 are flagged explicitly wherever they fall inside a fold's
validation window, rather than left to hide inside one aggregate number (the
ticket's own point 7: "one aggregate number will hide everything
interesting"). `kb/fold_spec.md` §7 already records that fold 5 never sees
2008 and the holdout is drawn from a calm regime; this reporting rule is what
makes those known gaps visible in the scorecard rather than only in the spec.
